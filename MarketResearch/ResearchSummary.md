# Research Summary: The State of Game Recommendation

## Executive Overview
Modern gamers are increasingly frustrated with "Storefront-style" discovery. They don't want to be sold the latest blockbuster; they want to find their next favorite experience. The primary "pain point" identified in research is the **gap between behavior and motivation.**

## Key Findings

1.  **The "Genre" Problem:** Broad genres are dead. Gamers now think in terms of "Game Loops" and "Moods." A recommendation engine that suggests *Call of Duty* because you played *DOOM* is seen as primitive. One that suggests *Ultrakill* because you enjoy "high-speed, movement-based shooters" is seen as valuable.
2.  **The Need for Agency:** Users want to "tune" their algorithm. They don't want a black box; they want to be the DJ of their own discovery feed.
3.  **The Shovelware Crisis:** The sheer volume of games (especially on platforms like Steam and itch.io) has made manual curation almost impossible. Users are looking for engines that act as a "Quality Filter" above all else.
4.  **Library Fragmentation:** Gamers play across PC, Console, and Handheld. An engine that doesn't "know" what the user already owns on other platforms is essentially useless for long-term discovery.

## Recommendation for Developers
To build a successful recommendation engine in 2026:
- Focus on **Metadata Quality:** Move beyond basic tags into mechanical and emotional descriptors.
- Implement **Robust Exclusions:** Respect the user's "No."
- Prioritize **Discovery over Sales:** Build trust by suggesting the *right* game, even if it's an old $5 indie title.
