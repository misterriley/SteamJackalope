# Video Game Recommendation Engines: Website Characteristics

This document details the website-level traits that help or hinder the game discovery process.

## 1. Good Characteristics (The "Gold Standard")

### Information Density and Clarity
- **Quick-Look Data:** Displaying essential info (price, platform, average playtime, and critic/user scores) without requiring a click.
- **Visual Previews:** High-quality, auto-playing (but muted) gameplay clips or "Before You Buy" style summaries.
- **Balanced Reviews:** Showcasing both the most helpful positive *and* negative reviews side-by-side to provide a realistic view.

### Advanced Filtering and Navigation
- **Granular Sorting:** Beyond just "Release Date" or "Price"; sorting by "Review Score Trend," "Weight of Mechanics," or "Similar to [Specific Game]."
- **Discovery-First Layouts:** Designing the UI to encourage browsing (e.g., "Infinite Scroll" done well or "Themed Collections") rather than just a search bar and a sales grid.
- **Mobile Responsiveness:** A large portion of gamers browse for their next game on mobile while away from their PC/Console.

### Trust and Verification
- **Verified Purchase Reviews:** Highlighting reviews from people who actually own and have played the game for a significant amount of time.
- **Curation:** Featured lists from trusted critics, influencers, or community experts rather than just algorithmic output.

---

## 2. Bad Characteristics (The "Red Flags")

### Bloat and Clutter
- **Shovelware Overload:** Failure to filter out "asset flips," low-effort clones, or "AI-generated junk" that buries legitimate indie titles.
- **Ad-Heavy Interfaces:** Websites that prioritize banner ads or "sponsored recommendations" over organic discovery.
- **DLC Fragmentation:** Allowing individual DLCs or microtransactions to take up main recommendation slots instead of the base game.

### Algorithmic Dark Patterns
- **Profit-First Bias:** Pushing games with high-profit margins or aggressive monetization rather than those the user would actually enjoy.
- **Engagement Traps:** Using "Fear of Missing Out" (FOMO) or countdown timers to pressure users into making quick decisions.

### Poor Search Functionality
- **Literal Search Failure:** Systems that can't handle typos or that fail to find a game unless the exact title is entered.
- **Broken Sorting:** Sorting options that don't work (e.g., "Sort by Price" showing free-to-play games with $100 DLCs at the top).

### Lack of Personalization
- **The "Guest" Experience:** Forcing users to create an account and manually enter data before showing *any* relevant recommendations.
- **One-Size-Fits-All Homepages:** Showing the exact same "Featured" games to every single visitor regardless of their history.
