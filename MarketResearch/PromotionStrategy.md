# Go-to-Market Strategy: Organic Growth for SteamJackalope

This document outlines a deep-dive strategy for promoting a free, utility-first game recommendation engine in 2026, focusing on community integration and organic discovery.

## 1. The "Hook": Mechanics-First Discovery
Since broad genres are failing, the marketing should lean into the **"How" and "Why" of gaming.**
*   **The USP:** Position the tool as a **"Game Logic Search Engine"** or a **"Mechanical Discovery Tool"** rather than a generic recommendation site.
*   **Proof of Value:** Use "Proof of Concept" storytelling.
    *   *Example:* "I was tired of Steam suggesting COD because I played DOOM, so I built a tool that found me Ultrakill based on 'movement-based mechanical loops.' Here’s how it works."

## 2. Strategic "Base Camps" (Community Integration)
Target environments where users are already expressing frustration with storefront discovery.

### Reddit (The Quality Filter)
*   **r/gamingsuggestions:** Monitor for specific/complex requests. Reply with helpful suggestions *and* mention the tool as a way to find more.
*   **r/patientgamers:** Pitch the tool as a way to find "lost classics" that match specific tastes, avoiding "trending loops."
*   **r/SideProject & r/webdev:** Use for technical feedback and finding early adopters who enjoy testing new web tools.

### Niche Hosting & Showcases
*   **Itch.io & Game Jolt:** Host the tool as a "Web Tool" project. This puts it directly in front of indie developers and "Hidden Gem" hunters.
*   **GitHub:** Keep a public-facing repository (even if just for the UI/Docs) to build trust with the developer community.

## 3. "Influencer" Outreach (The Niche Curators)
Focus on **"Library Architects"** and **"Hidden Gem Hunters"** rather than massive variety streamers.
*   **Target Creators:** Look for YouTube/Twitch channels dedicated to "Hidden Gems" or "Indie Spotlights" (e.g., *Splattercatgaming*, *ClemmyGames*).
*   **The Soft Pitch:** Send a short, personalized note: *"I made this free tool because I love your 'Hidden Gem' videos and wanted a way to find games with that same level of mechanical depth. No ads, just thought you might find it useful for your research."*

## 4. Content Strategy (Visual & Shareable)
Since the tool is visual (sliders, mechanics, explanations), the marketing must be visual.
*   **"The Slider GIF":** A 10-second GIF of a user adjusting a "Discovery Slider" from "Mainstream" to "Hidden Gem" with real-time result changes.
*   **"Because you liked..." Infographics:** Post side-by-side comparisons. *"Storefronts suggest [Popular Game] because you played [Game X]. My engine suggests [Indie Gem] because they both share [Specific Mechanical Loop]."*

## 5. Growth Loops (Self-Sustaining Discovery)
*   **Shareable Taste Profiles:** Allow users to generate a "Taste Card" or a "Discovery List" optimized for sharing on Discord, Reddit, or Threads.
*   **The "Anti-Storefront" Manifesto:** Include a "Why this exists" page on the site. Explain the fight against algorithmic bias to build community goodwill.

## 6. Summary Checklist for Launch
- [ ] **Polished Press Kit:** 3 high-res GIFs, a 30-second video walkthrough, and a 2-paragraph "Why."
- [ ] **Reddit Engagement:** Provide 5-10 high-value comments on r/gamingsuggestions before starting a dedicated thread.
- [ ] **Steam Curators:** Contact 5 curators who focus on niche mechanics or indie discovery.
- [ ] **Transparency:** Maintain a public dev log or "What's New" section to show active improvement.
